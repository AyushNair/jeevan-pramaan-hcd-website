Ideation & Solution

From Insights to Solutions

Brainstorming, evaluating, and selecting the most effective approach to solve Viswan's problem.

Problem Statement

"How might we help elderly pensioners understand why their photo verification failed, so they can fix the issue themselves without outside help?"

This "How Might We" statement was derived directly from our user research findings. Viswan's core frustration is not that verification fails — it's that he doesn't know why it fails and can't figure out how to fix it. Any solution must address both of these gaps.

Brainstorming: Ideas Explored

Through structured brainstorming, we generated five potential solutions to address the problem statement. Each idea was designed to improve the photo verification experience for elderly users:

  1. 1

    Plain-language rejection reason with specific fix instruction

    Replace the generic "Verification Failed" message with a specific, easy-to-understand reason (e.g., "Photo too dark — move to a brighter spot, like near a window") and a clear one-step instruction to fix it. The user sees exactly what went wrong and what to do next.

    ✓ SELECTED
  2. 2

    Voice-guided step-by-step photo process

    An audio assistant that speaks instructions aloud in the user's language: "Hold your phone at arm's length," "Make sure your face is in the oval," "Good, now hold still." Useful for users who struggle to read on-screen text, but requires significant development effort and multilingual support.

  3. 3

    Automatic photo quality check before submission

    Before the photo is submitted for verification, the app runs a local quality check — evaluating brightness, blur, face detection, and alignment. Only photos that pass the check are submitted. This prevents wasted attempts but requires on-device ML models and adds processing time.

  4. 4

    Video call with support agent for assistance

    A "Get Help" button that connects the user to a live support agent via video call. The agent can see the user's camera, guide them through the process, and even approve the certificate manually. Effective but extremely expensive to scale and requires infrastructure.

  5. 5

    Simplified face-alignment overlay with real-time feedback

    A face-shaped overlay on the camera screen that changes color (red → yellow → green) as the user positions their face correctly. Provides real-time visual feedback without text. Intuitive, but doesn't address post-submission errors and requires real-time face tracking.

Idea Selection Matrix

We evaluated each idea on four criteria, scoring each from 1 (low) to 5 (high). The idea with the highest total score was selected for prototyping.

Idea Feasibility Impact User Friendliness Low Cost Total
1. Plain-language rejection reason 5 5 5 5 20
2. Voice-guided process 3 4 5 2 14
3. Auto quality check 2 4 4 2 12
4. Video call support 2 3 3 1 9
5. Face-alignment overlay 3 3 4 3 13

Why Idea #1 Was Selected

Idea #1 — Plain-language rejection reasons with specific fix instructions — scored highest across all four criteria. Here's why it's the strongest solution:

✅ Highest Feasibility

It requires minimal backend changes. The verification system already knows why a photo failed (low brightness, face not detected, too much glare). The fix is simply surfacing this information to the user in clear language, rather than hiding it behind a generic error code.

✅ Maximum Impact

It directly addresses the core pain point from our empathy map. Viswan said, "I don't know what's wrong." This solution tells him exactly what's wrong and how to fix it. The bridge between failure and success becomes one simple instruction.

✅ Most User-Friendly

No new technology to learn. No voice interfaces. No video calls. The user simply reads one sentence — in plain language, not technical jargon — and follows one instruction. Even users with very low digital literacy can understand "Photo too dark — move to a brighter spot."

✅ Lowest Implementation Cost

Unlike voice guidance (which needs multilingual voice synthesis), auto quality checks (which need on-device ML), or video support (which needs a call center), this solution requires only a change to the error message UI. The intelligence is already in the backend — it just needs to be communicated.

Connecting Solution to Research

Empathy Map Finding

Says: "The photo looks fine to me — I don't know what's wrong with it."

Does: Retries the same photo repeatedly, guessing at changes.

Feels: Anxious about missing deadline, frustrated by lack of guidance.

How Our Solution Responds

Tells him exactly: "Photo too dark — move to a brighter spot, like near a window."

Enables: One specific action instead of random guessing.

Restores confidence: "The system is helping me. I can do this."
